Customer Support: interview questions and learning guide

Service skills and support operations

Practise Customer Support on Padimachi

What you will learn

Customer service skills

Every call is a chance to turn a problem into loyalty.

Good service listens first, shows empathy, solves the problem clearly and follows up. When a customer is upset, calm them before fixing. When you cannot solve it, escalate with full details.

Interview tip: Use the pattern: listen, empathise, solve, confirm.

Support operations

A well-run help desk is a calm kitchen at rush hour.

Support teams log every request in a ticketing system and work it by priority. First-line staff solve common issues, harder cases go to second line, and knowledge articles let customers help themselves.

Interview tip: Know your metrics: handle time, backlog, CSAT and SLA.

Ticketing and SLA management

A ticket is a receipt that promises the customer a tracked answer.

A ticketing tool records each customer issue with an owner, status and time. Tickets are classified, prioritised and routed to the right team. Each priority has response and resolution targets in an SLA. Agents update notes so anyone can continue the case. Managers watch queues and warn before targets are missed.

Interview tip: Show that you know the difference between response time and resolution time.

Handling difficult customers and escalations

An angry customer is usually a worried customer wearing a loud coat.

Stay calm first. Let the customer vent, then show you heard the problem. Take ownership and give clear next steps with times. If you cannot solve it, escalate with full notes so the customer does not repeat the story. Protect yourself from abuse by following the policy. Follow up after the fix.

Interview tip: Use the words 'I will' and give a time, because ownership calms people faster than excuses.

Voice, email and chat support etiquette

Each channel has its own manners, but all of them need clarity and respect.

On voice, tone and pace carry the message, so greet warmly and avoid long silence. In email, use a clear subject, short paragraphs and a complete answer. In chat, reply quickly in short lines and handle more than one chat with care. Never use slang or blame. Confirm understanding and close with a summary.

Interview tip: Compare how you would handle the same complaint on call, email and chat.

Knowledge base writing

A good help article answers the question so well that the ticket never gets raised.

A knowledge base stores tested answers for customers and agents. Each article should solve one problem in plain words. Use a clear title that matches how users search. Number the steps and add screenshots only where needed. Review articles often and retire old ones. Track which articles reduce tickets.

Interview tip: Show how you would test an article with a new colleague before publishing.

Support metrics: CSAT, NPS, FCR and AHT

Metrics are the speedometer of a support team; use them to steer, not to scare.

Support teams track speed, quality and satisfaction. Speed shows in handle time and response time. Quality shows in first contact fixes and quality scores. Satisfaction shows in short surveys. No metric stands alone: pushing handle time down alone can hurt quality. Leaders read metrics together and act on causes.

Interview tip: Explain how two metrics can pull against each other and how you would balance them.

Interview questions and sample answers

How do you handle a customer who is shouting?

Stay calm, let them finish, apologise and empathise, then solve or escalate and follow up.

How do you balance speed with quality on calls?

Use knowledge articles and checklists to resolve first time, since quality reduces repeat contacts.

A customer is angry on a call. What do you do?

Stay calm, let them finish, apologise for the experience, confirm the problem, give a clear action and time, and follow up.

What is first call resolution?

The share of customer issues solved on the first contact without a callback or transfer.

What is the difference between SLA and OLA?

An SLA is a promise to the customer about response and resolution. An OLA is an internal agreement between teams to support that promise.

How do you handle a ticket you cannot solve?

Gather details and steps tried, check the knowledge base, escalate to the right team with a clear summary and keep the customer updated.

What makes a good email reply to a customer?

A clear subject, thanks and acknowledgement, a direct answer or next step, simple words and a polite close with contact details.

What is CSAT and how do you improve it?

Customer satisfaction score from a short survey. Improve it with faster answers, correct first-time fixes, empathy and follow-ups.

What is the difference between priority and severity?

Severity is how bad the impact is. Priority is how soon it should be fixed based on business needs.

How do you stay productive on repetitive tickets?

Use templates and macros, learn the common causes, suggest fixes to the knowledge base and automate where possible.

How do you handle an angry customer?

Stay calm, listen fully, empathise, apologise for the experience, solve or escalate and follow up.

What is an SLA in customer support?

A promise about response and resolution times, such as replying within 4 hours.

How do you write a good support email?

Greet by name, acknowledge the issue, give clear steps, keep it short and polite, and state next steps.

What do you do when you do not know the answer?

Be honest, search the knowledge base, ask a colleague, tell the customer when you will reply and follow up.

What is CSAT and NPS?

CSAT measures satisfaction with one interaction. NPS measures how likely customers are to recommend you.

How do you prioritise many tickets?

By impact, urgency and SLA. Handle outages and VIP or blocked users first, and keep others updated.

How do you stay positive in repetitive support work?

Take breaks, look for patterns to suggest fixes, learn new skills and remember each solved case helps a person.

Senior and lead interview questions

How do you build a customer support team and its processes?

Define channels, SLAs, scripts and a knowledge base, hire for empathy, train well and review quality.

How do you improve customer satisfaction scores?

Analyse feedback, fix top causes, coach agents, speed up first response and close the loop with customers.

How do you handle team burnout in support?

Balance shifts, rotate hard queues, allow breaks, recognise effort and fix repeat problems at the source.

How do you decide what to automate with bots?

Pick high-volume simple questions, keep an easy route to a human and measure resolution and satisfaction.

How do you handle a public complaint on social media?

Reply quickly and politely, take details private, fix the issue and follow up publicly if needed.

How do you report support insights to product teams?

Tag tickets by cause, quantify the top issues, share customer quotes and propose fixes.

How do you set SLAs that are realistic?

Use past data, agree priorities, align with staffing and review misses to adjust.
