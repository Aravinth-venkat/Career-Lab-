HR & Recruitment: interview questions and learning guide

People, hiring, payroll and compliance

Practise HR & Recruitment on Padimachi

What you will learn

HR fundamentals

HR is the bridge between people and the business.

HR manages the employee lifecycle: attracting, hiring, developing, rewarding and retaining people. It also keeps the company compliant with labour laws and builds a healthy culture.

Interview tip: Show that you balance employee interests with business goals.

Recruitment & onboarding

Recruiting is matchmaking: the right person, the right role, the right time.

Recruitment starts with a clear job description, then sourcing, screening, interviews, offer and background checks. Onboarding helps the new hire become productive and stay.

Interview tip: Quote numbers: time to hire, offers accepted, source quality.

Performance & compliance

Fair pay and clear feedback keep a workplace running smoothly.

HR sets goals, reviews performance and manages payroll accurately and on time. Compliance means following labour laws and company policy so employees are protected and the company avoids penalties.

Interview tip: Accuracy and confidentiality are the two words to stress in payroll roles.

Talent acquisition and sourcing

Hiring is like fishing: you must know the pond, the bait and the fish you want.

Talent acquisition starts with a clear hiring need and a good job brief. Sourcers then find people through job portals, social networks, referrals and campuses. They screen profiles, call candidates and sell the role honestly. Good teams track every stage in an applicant tracking system and measure cost and speed. Strong sourcing builds a pipeline before the vacancy becomes urgent.

Interview tip: Explain how you source passive candidates, not only how you post jobs on portals.

Payroll and statutory compliance in India

Payroll is the monthly promise a company must keep exactly and on time.

Payroll turns attendance and pay terms into salary for each person. HR first fixes the salary structure with basic pay, allowances and deductions. Then it applies statutory rules such as provident fund, state insurance, gratuity, professional tax and tax deducted at source. Payments and returns must meet due dates set by each law. Records must be kept so an audit can prove every figure.

Interview tip: Say that you know rules and due dates change, and that you confirm them with official notices before every cycle.

Employee engagement and retention

People stay where they feel seen, useful and fairly treated.

Engagement is how much effort and care people give to their work. Retention is how long they stay. HR listens through surveys, stay talks and exit data. It finds the real causes such as poor managers, low growth or unfair pay. Then it acts with small fixes first, like better recognition and clearer career steps. Results must be tracked, or the survey becomes noise.

Interview tip: Show that you act on survey results, because collecting feedback without action hurts trust.

Learning and development

Training is a bridge between the skills people have and the skills the business needs.

Learning and development begins with a gap between current and needed skills. HR finds this gap through reviews, manager input and business goals. It then picks the right format such as classroom, online, coaching or on-the-job practice. After training, it checks if behaviour and results changed. Without that check, training is only a cost.

Interview tip: Always link a training plan to a business result, not only to hours of attendance.

HR analytics and HRIS

Data lets HR move from opinions to evidence.

An HRIS is the system of record for employee data, leave, payroll links and workflows. HR analytics uses that data to answer questions such as who is leaving and why. A good analyst first cleans data, then picks a few metrics tied to a decision. Charts must be simple enough for a manager to act on. Employee data is private, so access must be limited and logged.

Interview tip: Mention data privacy and who may see salary data when you talk about HR dashboards.

Interview questions and sample answers

Walk me through your recruitment process from job description to offer.

Clarify the role, source and screen candidates, run interviews, make the offer, verify and onboard.

How do you handle a grievance raised by an employee?

Listen, keep it confidential, investigate fairly, apply policy, resolve and follow up.

What are the steps of the recruitment process?

Workforce planning, job description, sourcing, screening, interviews, selection, offer, background check and onboarding.

How do you reduce employee attrition?

Find causes through exit interviews and surveys, then fix pay gaps, manager quality, growth paths, workload and recognition.

What is the difference between a PIP and a warning letter?

A PIP is a structured improvement plan with goals and support. A warning letter records a conduct or discipline issue formally.

Explain PF, ESI and gratuity in simple words.

PF is a retirement fund from employee and employer contributions. ESI gives medical and sickness benefits for lower-wage staff. Gratuity is a lump sum after five years of service, under the rules in force.

How do you handle a conflict between two employees?

Listen to both separately, find facts, hold a joint conversation, agree on actions and follow up. Escalate if policy is broken.

What is onboarding and why does it matter?

Onboarding is the first weeks of a new hire: paperwork, tools, introductions and goals. Good onboarding improves speed to productivity and retention.

What is a KPI versus a KRA?

A KRA is an area of responsibility. A KPI is a measurable number that shows progress in that area.

How do you calculate attrition rate?

Employees who left in a period divided by the average headcount in that period, multiplied by 100.

Walk me through the recruitment process from requisition to joining.

Approve the opening, write the job description, source candidates, screen, interview, make the offer, do background checks and onboarding.

What are the main labour laws an HR executive should know in India?

Examples are the Shops and Establishments rules, Payment of Wages, PF and ESI, Gratuity, and the POSH Act. Rules change, so confirm current law.

What is the POSH Act?

The law on preventing sexual harassment at the workplace. It needs an Internal Committee, a clear policy and training.

How do you reduce attrition?

Find reasons through exit and stay interviews, fix pay or manager issues, give growth paths, recognise good work and onboard well.

What is the difference between CTC and in-hand salary?

CTC is the total cost to the company including employer PF and benefits. In-hand is what you receive after deductions like PF, tax and insurance.

What KPIs do you track in HR?

Examples are time to hire, offer acceptance rate, attrition, absenteeism and training hours. Pick those that link to business goals.

How do you conduct an effective interview?

Prepare structured questions, ask for examples, take notes, score against criteria and avoid bias.

Senior and lead interview questions

How do you build an HR strategy aligned with business goals?

Understand business plans, find talent gaps, set priorities for hiring, retention and learning, and measure with clear metrics.

How do you handle a layoff or restructuring fairly?

Follow law and policy, use clear criteria, communicate with respect, offer support such as notice and outplacement, and protect remaining morale.

How do you reduce attrition in a team that is losing people?

Find causes from exit and stay interviews, fix manager and pay issues, offer growth paths and recognition.

How do you design a performance management system?

Set clear goals, regular feedback, fair calibration, development plans and training for managers.

How do you handle a harassment complaint?

Take it seriously, follow the POSH process and Internal Committee, protect confidentiality, investigate fairly and act on findings.

How do you build a strong employer brand?

Share real employee stories, treat candidates well, keep promises, and measure offer acceptance and referrals.

How do you advise leaders on compensation design?

Use market data, internal fairness, pay bands, performance link, and clear communication of the structure.
