Interview & Communication: interview questions and learning guide

Self-introduction, STAR, English and workplace skills

What you will learn

Self-introduction & STAR

Your introduction is the trailer of your career: short, clear, and it makes them want more.

A strong self-introduction follows present, past, future: who you are now, what you have done and what you want next. For behavioural questions use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result, and spend most of your time on the action and result.

Interview tip: Keep your introduction to about 60 seconds and end with your goal.

Common HR questions

Every HR question is really asking: will you fit, will you grow, will you stay?

HR rounds test attitude and fit. Prove strengths with examples, show a real weakness you are fixing, give a researched salary range, explain gaps honestly and never criticise a former employer.

Interview tip: Prepare a one-line answer plus one proof for each common question.

Communication & English tips

Clear speaking is a skill you can train like a muscle.

Speak in short clear sentences, pause between ideas and avoid filler sounds. Listen fully before answering, paraphrase to confirm and match your tone and body language to your words.

Interview tip: Practise aloud with the Speak tab and record your pace.

Workplace skills

Skills get you hired; habits keep you growing.

Employers value people who cooperate, manage time, handle conflict calmly, accept feedback and take initiative. Show these with short real stories rather than adjectives.

Interview tip: Replace adjectives with proof: a number, a name, an outcome.

Group discussion and presentations

A group discussion is a cricket match: you score by good shots, not by hogging the bat.

In a group discussion, assessors watch how you think, listen and speak with others. Start with a short, clear point, build on what others said and bring the group back when it drifts. Facts and examples beat loud opinions. In presentations, keep a simple structure of opening, three points and a close, use few words per slide and speak to the audience, not the screen. Practice and timing matter more than fancy design.

Interview tip: Aim for quality of points and calm tone, not the number of times you speak.

Handling stress and conflict at work

Stress is a pot on the stove: you must lower the heat or let steam out before it boils over.

Pressure and disagreement are normal at work, and interviewers want to hear how you handle them. For stress, list tasks, rank them, ask for help early and take short breaks. For conflict, stay calm, speak privately, listen to the other view and focus on the problem, not the person. Look for a shared goal and agree on next steps. Involve the manager only when direct talks fail.

Interview tip: Use the structure situation, action, result, and avoid blaming anyone in your story.

Salary negotiation and offer discussion

Negotiating is a polite exchange where both sides want to say yes.

A job offer is more than one number. Understand fixed pay, variable pay, bonus, benefits, notice period and joining date. Research the market range for your role and city, and know your own minimum. Let the employer share a figure first when possible, then reply with a researched range and reasons such as skills and results. Stay warm, never give ultimatums and get the final offer in writing before resigning.

Interview tip: Say what you bring, not what you need, and keep the tone friendly all the way through.

Interview questions and sample answers

How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?

Clarify deadlines and impact, rank by urgency and importance, tell stakeholders the plan and ask for help on trade-offs.

How do you give feedback to a colleague?

Be specific and kind, describe the behaviour and its effect, suggest a change and ask for their view in private.

How do you receive criticism?

Listen without defending, ask for examples, thank the person, decide what to change and follow up.

How do you manage your time?

Plan the day, set top three priorities, block focus time, limit interruptions and review at day end.

How do you work with a difficult team member?

Understand their view, focus on the work not the person, agree on clear expectations and involve a manager if it continues.

Describe how you learn a new skill quickly.

Set a goal, learn the basics, build a small project, ask for feedback and practise weekly.

How do you speak up in a meeting if you are shy?

Prepare one point in advance, speak early, use short sentences, and ask a question if you are not ready to offer an opinion.

How do you handle a mistake you made at work?

Tell the right person quickly, explain facts without excuses, fix it, and add a check so it does not repeat.
