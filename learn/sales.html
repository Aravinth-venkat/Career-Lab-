Sales & Marketing: interview questions and learning guide

Selling, digital marketing and customers

Practise Sales & Marketing on Padimachi

What you will learn

Sales process

Selling is solving a problem for someone, not pushing a product.

A sale moves from lead to qualified lead, then to a proposal and closing. Good sellers ask questions to understand the need, handle objections with facts and empathy, and follow up consistently.

Interview tip: Ask more questions than you answer, and quote your quota achievement.

Digital marketing

Marketing is being useful in the places your customers already look.

Digital marketing attracts people through search, social media, email and content. SEO earns free traffic, SEM buys it, and analytics shows which efforts lead to conversions and at what cost.

Interview tip: Tie every channel to a number: clicks, leads or cost per customer.

CRM & customer relationships

Keeping a customer is cheaper than finding a new one.

A CRM stores every interaction so teams know the history. Strong account managers measure retention and churn, ask for feedback, and grow accounts with relevant upsell and cross-sell offers.

Interview tip: Mention retention and satisfaction results to show long-term thinking.

Lead generation and prospecting

Prospecting is planting seeds so the pipeline has something to grow.

Lead generation finds people who may need your product. Prospecting qualifies them and starts a talk. Start with an ideal customer profile so effort goes to the right companies. Mix inbound sources like web forms with outbound work like calls and email. Keep messages short and about the buyer's problem. Track every touch so no lead is lost.

Interview tip: Describe your ideal customer profile and how you decide a lead is worth chasing.

Negotiation and closing

Negotiation is not a fight; it is a swap that leaves both sides better off.

Negotiation begins before the meeting with a clear goal, a limit and options. In the meeting, listen for what the buyer values most. Trade, do not give: every concession should earn something in return. Protect value by showing outcomes before discussing price. Close by summarising what is agreed and asking for the next step. Confirm in writing.

Interview tip: Say you trade concessions for something, never give discounts for free.

B2B account management

A key account is a long marriage, not a one-night sale.

Account management grows and protects revenue from existing business customers. The manager learns the customer's goals and maps all the people who influence decisions. A written account plan lists targets, risks and actions. Regular reviews show value delivered. Early work on renewals and extra needs keeps the account safe from rivals.

Interview tip: Show that you start renewal work months early and that you track more than one contact per account.

SEO, SEM, social media and content marketing

Marketing channels are different roads that lead people to the same shop.

Digital marketing uses search, paid ads, social posts and content to reach buyers. SEO earns free search visibility through useful pages and good structure. SEM buys visibility through paid search ads. Social media builds reach and trust. Content marketing answers buyer questions and feeds all channels. Each channel needs a goal, a budget and a way to measure results.

Interview tip: Pick one channel and explain how you would measure its return in numbers.

Sales analytics and forecasting

A forecast is a weather report for revenue: useful only if the method is honest.

Sales analytics studies data from the CRM to see what drives wins. Funnel metrics show where deals drop. Forecasting predicts revenue from the pipeline using stage chances and past results. Good forecasts compare predicted and actual figures and learn from gaps. Clean CRM data is the base for everything.

Interview tip: Say that your forecast is only as good as the data entry discipline behind it.

Interview questions and sample answers

How do you handle a customer who says your price is too high?

Understand the concern, reinforce value and benefits, compare with alternatives, and offer options before closing.

How do you build and manage your sales pipeline?

Generate and qualify leads, follow up consistently, track stages in a CRM and forecast against target.

How do you qualify a lead?

Check need, budget, authority and timeline. Ask questions to confirm they are a real fit before spending time on a full pitch.

How do you handle the objection 'your price is too high'?

Ask what they compare with, restate the value and results, show cost of not solving the problem, and offer options without discounting too fast.

Describe your sales process from lead to close.

Prospect, qualify, discover needs, present, handle objections, negotiate, close, then hand over and follow up for repeat business.

How do you hit a target when the month is slow?

Review the pipeline, focus on the deals most likely to close, follow up old leads, ask for referrals and plan daily activity numbers.

What is a sales funnel?

The journey from awareness to interest, decision and purchase. Each stage loses some people, so you track conversion at each step.

How do you build a relationship with a customer who does not reply?

Vary the channel and timing, add value with useful information, keep messages short and give a clear next step.

What is the difference between B2B and B2C sales?

B2B sells to businesses with longer cycles and several decision makers. B2C sells to individuals with faster, emotional decisions.

How do you measure your sales performance?

Revenue against target, conversion rate, average deal size, pipeline coverage, call and meeting counts and customer retention.

What is your sales process from lead to close?

Prospect, qualify, present, handle objections, propose, negotiate, close and follow up for repeat business.

How do you build a pipeline?

Mix inbound leads, outbound calls and referrals, and track stages and conversion so you know what to fix.

How do you deal with a missed target?

Review numbers honestly, find the cause, make a recovery plan with daily activity goals and ask for coaching.

What is upselling versus cross-selling?

Upselling offers a higher version of what they buy. Cross-selling offers a related product.

How do you follow up without being annoying?

Add value each time, use different channels, space the messages and respect a clear no.

What CRM have you used and why does it matter?

Name the tool, such as Zoho or Salesforce, and say it keeps leads, notes and next steps organised so no deal is forgotten.

Senior and lead interview questions

How do you build and coach a sales team?

Set clear targets, teach a repeatable process, run call reviews, coach weekly and recognise wins.

How do you forecast sales accurately?

Use pipeline stages with conversion rates, review deals weekly, be honest about risk and track forecast accuracy.

How do you handle a key account that threatens to leave?

Meet the customer, listen to the issue, fix root causes, offer a recovery plan and add senior sponsors.

How do you design sales territories and quotas?

Use market potential, past data and capacity to balance territories, and set quotas that are stretching but fair.

How do you grow revenue from existing customers?

Map accounts, find needs, cross-sell and upsell, review value regularly and add customer success touchpoints.

How do you handle an underperforming salesperson?

Review activity and skills, give clear targets and coaching, set a time-bound plan and decide fairly.

How do you work with marketing and product?

Share feedback from customers, agree lead definitions, review funnel metrics together and align on messages.
