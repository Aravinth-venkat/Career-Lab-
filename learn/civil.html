Civil & Construction: interview questions and learning guide

Structures, concrete, site work, surveying and soil

Practise Civil & Construction on Padimachi

What you will learn

Structural basics

Every building is a conversation between loads and the paths that carry them to the ground.

Structures carry dead loads from their own weight and live loads from use, plus wind and earthquake loads. Beams bend and shear, columns carry compression, and reinforced concrete uses steel bars to take tension. Designers keep deflection and stress within code limits.

Interview tip: Mention the codes and software you used, such as IS codes, STAAD or ETABS.

Materials & site management

On site, good planning and good records decide whether the design becomes a good building.

Concrete quality depends on the mix, the water-cement ratio, placing, compaction and curing. Site engineers run slump and cube tests, check reinforcement against drawings, measure quantities for the bill of quantities and keep safety and progress records.

Interview tip: Give numbers from your sites: quantities, crew size, schedule.

Surveying, soil & transport

Before anything is built, someone has to know exactly where the ground is and what it can hold.

Surveyors measure heights and positions with levels and total stations from fixed benchmarks. Geotechnical engineers study soil to find its bearing capacity and settlement behaviour and choose a suitable foundation. Compaction and drainage keep roads and platforms stable.

Interview tip: Explain how you check your own measurements.

RCC design basics and reinforcement detailing

Concrete is strong when squeezed and weak when stretched, so steel is placed where the stretching happens.

Reinforced concrete combines concrete for compression and steel bars for tension. Limit state design checks strength at ultimate load and checks cracking and deflection at working load. Beams carry bending with main bars at the tension face and shear with stirrups. Detailing decides if the design works on site: cover, spacing, anchorage, laps and bar cut-off must all follow the drawings and the code.

Interview tip: Explain that detailing errors cause more site failures than calculation errors.

Estimating and costing, BOQ

Estimating is like a shopping list for a building: count everything first, then put a price on each line.

Estimating predicts the cost of work before it is built. The estimator takes quantities from drawings, writes them in a measurement sheet, and applies rates found by rate analysis or a schedule of rates. Rates cover material, labour, equipment, wastage, overheads and profit. The result is a bill of quantities used for tenders. During construction, actual work is measured and billed in running bills against agreed items.

Interview tip: Always mention checking deductions and units, as mistakes there change totals sharply.

Building codes, drawings and site supervision

Codes are the rules of the game and drawings are the playbook: the site engineer makes sure both are followed.

Building codes set minimum rules for safety, fire, structure, light and services, and local authorities check plans against them. Site engineers read plans, sections and details, set out work, and check each stage before the next begins. They keep records of inspections, tests and changes. At the end, as-built drawings and a snag list are prepared before the building is handed over and occupied.

Interview tip: Say you never allow concreting without checking reinforcement and recording approval.

Construction planning and scheduling

A schedule is a road map: it shows the route, the stops that cannot move and where you can lose time safely.

Planning breaks a project into activities, links them in logical order, assigns durations and resources, and finds the critical path. Activities with float can slip a little, while critical ones cannot. Planners show this on bar charts and track progress weekly with short lookahead plans. If work falls behind, they can add resources or overlap tasks, each with a cost or risk. Earned value compares progress against plan.

Interview tip: Mention updating the programme weekly with actual progress and showing the effect of delays.

Environmental and water engineering basics

Water engineering is plumbing at city scale: clean water comes in, used water goes out, and nature must not be harmed.

Water supply systems collect, treat and distribute water, with demand worked out from population and per capita use. Treatment steps include screening, coagulation, settling, filtration and disinfection. Wastewater is collected and treated to reduce organic load before release. Storm drains are sized from rainfall and runoff, and rainwater harvesting recharges groundwater. Projects often need environmental approvals and monitoring.

Interview tip: Know the treatment sequence in order and say why each step exists.

Interview questions and sample answers

How do you check the quality of concrete on site?

Run slump tests on fresh concrete, cast cubes for 7 and 28 day strength, check the mix and make sure curing is done properly.

Explain dead load and live load.

Dead load is the permanent weight of the structure and live load is temporary load from use, and design codes give values for both.

How do you plan a site activity such as a slab pour?

Check drawings and approvals, arrange material, labour and equipment, inspect reinforcement and formwork, and follow safety rules.

What is a bill of quantities and how is it used?

It lists work items with quantities and rates, and is used for cost estimates, tenders and billing based on measured work.

How do you deal with a delay on site?

Find the cause, check the critical path, add resources or change sequence, inform the client and track the recovery plan.

What is the difference between dead load and live load?

Dead load is the permanent weight of the structure. Live load is movable, like people and furniture.

What is the water cement ratio and why does it matter?

The weight of water divided by cement. A lower ratio gives higher strength and durability, as long as the mix stays workable.

What is the purpose of curing concrete?

To keep it moist and warm so cement hydrates fully, giving strength and reducing cracks.

What is a bar bending schedule?

A table listing each reinforcement bar, shape, length, number and weight for fabrication and checking.

What are the types of surveying instruments you have used?

Levels, theodolites, total stations and GPS. Mention what you measured and how you checked accuracy.

What is the standard penetration test?

A soil test that counts hammer blows to drive a sampler a set depth, used to judge soil strength.

How do you check the quality of bricks and cement on site?

Check brand and batch, visual and size checks, absorption and strength tests as per specification, and keep records.

What safety steps do you take before excavation?

Check underground services, support the sides, keep material away from the edge, provide access and barriers, and inspect daily.
